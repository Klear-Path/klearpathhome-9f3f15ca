# Right-to-Know Law requests — advocate/attestation eligibility

Two ready-to-send requests. Pennsylvania's Right-to-Know Law (Act 3 of 2008) gives an
agency **five business days** to respond in writing: grant, deny with a legal basis and
appeal rights, or invoke a 30-day extension. Silence is itself a deemed denial you can
appeal to the Office of Open Records at no cost. That is the difference between this and
a phone call.

You are not asking for a favor or an opinion. You are requesting records that already
exist — the agency's own written guidance on who may sign. Keep it to records; a request
phrased as a question ("am I eligible?") can be legitimately refused, because the RTKL
covers records, not answers.

**Before sending:** fill in the bracketed fields. Send by email and keep the sent copy.
Note the date you sent it and count five business days.

## If they miss the deadline

A non-response within five business days is a deemed denial. You may appeal to the
**Pennsylvania Office of Open Records** within 15 business days, at no cost, at
openrecords.pa.gov. Appeals are decided on the written record — no lawyer, no hearing,
no filing fee. Agencies that ignore email tend to answer an OOR docket number.
